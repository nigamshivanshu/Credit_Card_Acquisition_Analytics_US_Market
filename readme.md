# Customer Acquisition & Growth Analytics  
## US Credit Card Business (End-to-End Analytics Case Study)

---

## 📌 Project Overview

This project is an end-to-end **customer acquisition analytics case study**, designed to simulate how a US-based credit card business evaluates acquisition efficiency, marketing spend effectiveness, and early customer value.

The analysis focuses on **business decision-making**, not just reporting.  
All data processing, validation, analysis, insights, and recommendations are documented step-by-step in a single Jupyter Notebook, mirroring how analytics work is reviewed internally by business and strategy teams.

---

## 🎯 Business Objective

Acquisition volume alone does not guarantee profitable growth.

The objective of this project is to:
- Identify **where customers drop off** in the acquisition funnel
- Evaluate **marketing spend efficiency** across acquisition channels
- Compare **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)** against early customer value
- Support a **value-driven acquisition strategy** rather than a volume-driven one

---

## 🧠 Key Business Questions

- Where are the largest drop-offs in the acquisition funnel?
- Which acquisition channels are most cost-efficient?
- How does customer quality differ across channels and segments?
- Does low CAC always translate into higher customer value?
- What strategic actions can improve acquisition ROI without increasing spend?

---

## 📊 Analytical Scope

The project covers the full acquisition and early customer lifecycle:

- Applications → Approvals → Activations (Acquisition Funnel)
- Channel-level conversion and drop-off analysis
- Marketing spend attribution and CAC calculation
- Customer-level revenue aggregation
- Early LTV proxy estimation
- Segment-level customer quality analysis
- Executive-level insights and recommendations

---

## 🧮 Dataset Design

The analysis uses **large-scale synthetic data** (hundreds of thousands of rows) designed to closely resemble real-world credit card acquisition data.

Key characteristics:
- Multiple acquisition channels (Digital, Partner, Referral, Organic)
- Realistic funnel drop-offs
- Skewed transaction and revenue distributions
- Missing values, outliers, and noise to reflect production data quality challenges

This allows the analysis to focus on **realistic trade-offs**, not idealized outcomes.

---

## 🔍 Methodology

1. Business problem framing and KPI definition  
2. Relational data design and validation  
3. Data quality checks and controlled cleaning  
4. Acquisition funnel analysis by channel  
5. Marketing spend and CAC analysis  
6. Revenue, customer quality, and early LTV evaluation  
7. Strategic interpretation and executive recommendations  

An **Executive Summary** is placed at the top of the notebook to ensure stakeholder readability.

---

## 📈 Key Insights

- Activation (post-approval) is the largest bottleneck in the acquisition funnel  
- Channels with similar conversion rates can have very different CAC  
- Low CAC does not necessarily imply high customer value  
- A subset of customers and segments drives disproportionate early revenue  
- Optimizing for value can improve ROI without increasing acquisition spend  

---

## 🧭 Strategic Recommendations

- Prioritize activation optimization over increasing application volume  
- Adopt a value-adjusted acquisition strategy balancing CAC and early LTV  
- Reallocate spend toward channels with stronger cost–value balance  
- Refine targeting toward higher-value customer segments
